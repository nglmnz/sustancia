Pedagogical model

Statistical operations, interpretive depth, and the multidimensional mastery profile proposed by Sustancia.
Pedagogical architecture · evolving design

Every learner starts from a different place

Knowing whether an answer is right or wrong is not enough. Sustancia seeks to understand the concepts a student uses, the statistical operations they can perform, how they interpret findings, and what they can revise after feedback.

The mastery profile

One student might calculate a mean correctly but struggle to explain its significance. Another may interpret a distribution convincingly but need help calculating standard deviation. A single score would conceal these differences.

We therefore propose a multidimensional mastery profile: an evolving estimate based on evidence of specific abilities. It is neither a grade nor a psychological diagnosis.

Statistical concepts

Knowledge of variables, measures, distributions, sampling, and probability.

The system should distinguish estimated mastery, available evidence, and information not yet observed. A missing value is not the same as a zero score or a demonstrated weakness.

The statistical operations laboratory

Statistics is not a single skill. We propose eight families of operations that can be combined within a learning activity.

Operation What the student does
Recognize Identifies variables, units of analysis, populations, and samples.
Describe Characterizes a distribution or pattern in data.
Calculate Obtains measures and results using appropriate methods.
Compare Contrasts groups, measures, and indicators.
Interpret Explains what a statistical result means.
Contextualize Connects findings to the substantive phenomenon and its context.
Critique Examines assumptions, limitations, and competing explanations.
Transfer Applies a learned principle in a different situation.

Consider the mean: calculating it is only one task. We can also ask what it represents, when it might be misleading, how it differs from the median, what happens after removing outliers, or what conclusions it does not support. These operations are neither a rigid hierarchy nor mutually exclusive categories.

Interpretive depth: N1–N4

The original N1–N4 progression remains part of Sustancia, but it is now one dimension of the mastery profile, not the entire model.

Level Guiding question
N1 · Technical reading What is displayed in the data, table, or graph?
N2 · Statistical interpretation What relationship, magnitude, or difference is expressed?
N3 · Substantive interpretation What does this mean for the phenomenon being studied?
N4 · Critical and reflective reading What assumptions and limitations shape the conclusion?

These levels draw on research into reading representations and statistical reasoning (Curcio, Shaughnessy, Garfield and Ben-Zvi). The specific Sustancia framework still requires validation; it is not a universally validated scale. Points in the current game are not mastery percentages.

Epistemological conversations

Sources and statistical representations can become material for dialogue. Instead of providing answers, the system asks students to examine their own claims.

Source and problemWhat is the text or chart claiming?

Identify the claim, its source, and the problem it seeks to address. The academic team reviews the materials used for activities.

PositionWhat is your interpretation?

Students take a position and explain, in their own words, why it seems reasonable.

ContrastWhat evidence might change your mind?

A counterexample, measurement discrepancy, omitted assumption, or limitation challenges the initial explanation.

TransferDoes the principle hold in another context?

Students explore the concept in health, surveys, industrial production, or social indicators.

Application and revisionHow would you rewrite your answer?

The student creates another interpretation. The system can capture changes, persistent errors, and possible next steps.

How learners use feedback can be studied as observable revision quality. We do not infer frustration, resilience, or psychological traits from a single attempt.

Errors as learning opportunities

An error can guide the next question rather than merely reduce a score. The initial prototype’s taxonomy includes unjustified causal inference, overgeneralization, ecological fallacy, and reification of categories. Foundational statistics may add confusion between frequencies and percentages, mean and median, or population parameters and sample statistics. A unified classification must be established before connecting errors to a real mastery profile.

How might the next challenge be selected?

The proposed architecture combines the course curriculum, a validated activity bank, and evidence from the student’s mastery profile. An explicit pedagogical engine guides progression. AI can support retrieval, evaluation, and feedback, but it does not independently determine the curriculum or replace faculty oversight.

The team collects and reviews course materials. Students do not upload textbooks or PDF documents to play.
